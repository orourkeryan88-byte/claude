# Calling Meta Ad Leads — Southline Agency

Ryan's script for ringing leads that came in through the Facebook form.

---

## BEFORE YOU DIAL

Ring within five minutes. Connect rates collapse after that — the same
lead an hour later is a worse lead.

Newest first, always. Never work the list oldest to newest.

Have their name, their trade if you have it, and nothing else. Don't
research them. Ring them.

Stand up. It changes your voice.

Assume they do not remember filling the form. Half of them won't.

---

## THE OPENING

Change the opening depending on how old the lead is. A line that works
at four minutes sounds mad at four days.

**Under ten minutes old:**

> "Is that [Name]? — Ryan here. You were just after filling something
> in about the old customers sitting in your system. Have you two
> minutes?"

**Later the same day:**

> "Is that [Name]? — Ryan here from Southline. You put your details in
> earlier about the old contacts sitting in your system. Is now bad?"

**A day or more old:**

> "Is that [Name]? — Ryan from Southline. You filled something in on
> Facebook about your old customer list, I'm only getting to you now.
> Have you a minute?"

Why these work: you lead with what *they* did, not who you are. You are
returning their enquiry, not cold-calling them. Say your company name
second, never first — "Hi, I'm Ryan from Southline Agency and we help
businesses…" is a sales call and they will treat it as one.

"Have you two minutes?" and "Is now bad?" both make it easy to say no,
which is exactly why people say yes.

Never open with "How are you today?" It announces a sales call before
you have said anything.

**If they don't remember:**

> "No bother — it was the one about the contacts in your system that
> never got followed up. Ring any bell?"

Light and quick. Never defensive, never "well you DID fill it in".

**If it's genuinely a bad time:**

> "No problem — when's better, this evening or tomorrow morning?"

Two options. Get the time. Get off the phone.

**If a receptionist or someone else answers:**

> "Hi — is [Name] about? He was on to us earlier about the customer
> list." Then wait. You are returning a call, so say it like one.

---

## DISCOVERY

Seven questions, roughly in this order. Not an interrogation — if they
answer two at once, skip ahead. You are listening, not reading.

Rule: ask, then shut up. If you are talking more than they are, you
have stopped doing discovery and started pitching.

**1. Motive — why they filled it in**

> "So I'll not keep you — what made you fill that in?"

Then stop talking. Whatever they say next is what you sell to for the
rest of the call. This is the most commonly ruined line in the script.
Do not fill the pause.

*Listening for:* the words they use for their own problem. Write them
down and use them back later.

**2. Situation — what the business is**

> "And what is it you do yourself?"

*Listening for:* whether they're a fit at all, and what a customer is
likely worth. A clinic and a barber are very different calls.

**3. Scale — the size of the list**

> "What have you got sitting in there roughly — few hundred, few
> thousand?"

*Listening for:* under about five hundred and the maths gets thin. Not
a disqualifier, but price accordingly.

**4. Current state — what happens to those contacts now**

> "And what happens with them at the minute?"

Nearly everyone says "nothing, sure they're just sitting there." That
admission is the single most useful sentence in the call. Let them say
it — never say it for them.

*Listening for:* whether anyone follows up at all, and who.

**5. Value — what a customer is worth**

> "What's a customer worth to you over a year, give or take?"

If they hedge: "Rough is fine — hundred quid? Thousand?" Give them
brackets and they'll correct you, which is easier than making them do
sums on the spot.

*Listening for:* the number you'll use in the maths moment below.

**6. Why now — what changed**

> "And why now? You've had that list a while."

*Listening for:* urgency. A quiet month, a competitor, a target,
staff leaving. No answer here usually means no real urgency, which
means a longer sale — worth knowing before you push for a close.

**7. Capacity — could they even handle it**

> "If twenty of them came back to you next month, could you look after
> them?"

*Listening for:* an honest no. If they physically cannot service more
customers, do not sell them lead generation — sell them nothing, or
sell them the AI receptionist instead. A client who can't deliver
churns in two months and tells people it didn't work.

**Then, only if it's not obvious:**

> "Is it yourself that'd make the call on something like this, or is
> there someone else involved?"

Ask it plainly. Discovering a business partner at the closing stage is
how deals die.

---

Seven questions, four or five minutes, and you know their motive, their
trade, their list size, their economics, their urgency and whether they
can actually deliver. That is enough to close on.

---

## THE NUMBER MOMENT

Do the sum out loud using **their** figures. Never your own.

> "So if even a hundred of those two thousand came back to you, that's
> eighty grand. And they're sitting there doing nothing."

Say it and stop. Let the silence do the work. They are doing the maths
and it is landing harder than anything you could add.

---

## THE PITCH — TWO SENTENCES

> "So what we'd do is go back through that list and get the ones still
> in market talking to you again. Takes about a fortnight to set up."

That is the entire pitch. Any more and you are handing them objections
they didn't have.

Then:

> "Worth looking at properly?"

---

## CLOSING

If they're warm, do not book a second call. You are already on the
phone with them.

> "I've twenty minutes now — will I just walk you through how it'd work
> for your setup?"

If they can't talk:

> "Thursday morning at eleven, or Friday at two — which suits?"

Two options only. Confirm once. Then get off the phone.

---

## WHEN THEY PUSH BACK

**"How much is it?"**

> "Depends on the size of the list — for two thousand you'd be looking
> at a few hundred a month. I'd want to see what's actually in there
> before I put a number on it, that's what the twenty minutes is for."

Give a real ballpark. Dodging price on a live call reads as shifty.

**"I've tried something like this before."**

> "What happened?"

Listen properly. Do not defend, do not compare yourself favourably to
whoever burned them. When they finish:

> "Yeah, that'd put me off too. Can I show you why this is different,
> or would you rather I left it?"

**"Send me some information."**

> "I can, but there's nothing I could send that'd be much use — it
> depends entirely on what's in your list. Twenty minutes and you'd
> know for certain. Thursday?"

**"I need to think about it."**

> "Course. What's the bit you're unsure about?"

Never accept this as final without one question. The real objection is
behind it.

**"I'm too busy."**

> "That's the whole point of it — it runs without you. Fifteen minutes,
> early morning or late evening?"

**Third pushback with no movement — take it away:**

> "Look, it might not be for you. It works best for people already
> getting enquiries they haven't the time to chase. Will I leave it
> with you?"

Most people come back at that. If they don't, they were never buying.
Thank them and move to the next name.

---

## NO ANSWER

**Voicemail, under fifteen seconds:**

> "[Name], Ryan from Southline — you filled in a form on Facebook. I'll
> try you again later, or ring me back on [number]. Thanks."

Never pitch a voicemail. Its only job is making your name familiar for
the next attempt.

**Text straight after:**

> "Hi [Name] — Ryan from Southline, tried you there about the form you
> filled in on Facebook. When suits for a quick chat?"

The text is what gets the callback. Most people never listen to the
voicemail.

**Cadence:** ring again that evening, once the next morning, once the
following afternoon. Five attempts across three days, then park them
for ninety days. Don't burn the number.

---

## THE TWO RULES

**Say less than they do.** If you're talking more than half the call
you're pitching instead of qualifying, and pitching is what kills these
calls.

**Never discount to rescue a call.** At three or four euro a lead you
can afford to lose one. Dropping your price to save a wobbly prospect
trains you to sell cheap and gets you the worst clients you will ever
have.
