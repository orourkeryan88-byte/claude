# KPIs, Targets, and What We Actually Report

## The hierarchy — report top-down, diagnose bottom-up

**Tier 1 — business outcomes (what the client cares about)**
Revenue attributed · Customer acquisition cost · Return on ad spend · LTV:CAC ratio · Payback period

**Tier 2 — funnel efficiency (where the problem is)**
Cost per lead · Lead-to-sale conversion rate · Landing page conversion rate · Show rate (for call funnels) · AOV

**Tier 3 — channel mechanics (how we fix it)**
CPM · CTR · Hook rate · Frequency · Impression share · Quality score · Creative-level CPA

Clients get Tier 1 and 2 on page one. Tier 3 lives in the appendix and in our own dashboards.

## Benchmarks to sanity-check against
These are starting reference points, not promises — every vertical differs and you should replace them with the client's own historical data as soon as you have 90 days of it.

| Metric | Typical healthy range |
|---|---|
| Landing page conversion (lead magnet) | 20–40% |
| Landing page conversion (book-a-call) | 2–8% |
| Ecommerce site conversion | 1–3% |
| Email open rate | 25–45% |
| Lead-to-sale (inbound, fast follow-up) | 10–30% |
| LTV:CAC | 3:1 or better |

## Attribution honesty
- Platform-reported conversions are inflated; GA4 under-reports; the client's CRM/bank is the truth.
- Report platform numbers *and* client-confirmed sales side by side. Explain the gap once, in plain English, and then keep the same method every month.
- Use a consistent UTM convention from day one: `utm_source / utm_medium / utm_campaign / utm_content / utm_term`. Document it and never change it mid-quarter.
- Add a "How did you hear about us?" field to forms. Imperfect, but it catches what pixels miss.

## Dashboard spec
One page, live, shared with the client: spend · leads · cost per lead · qualified leads · sales · CAC · revenue · ROAS, each with month-to-date, target line, and previous period. Anything requiring a login the client doesn't have doesn't count as reporting.
