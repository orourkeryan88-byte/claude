Your ideal client is a bathroom fitter

The profile

Owner-operated bathroom installation specialists, £400k–£1.5m turnover, 4–15 staff, 3+ years trading, serving homeowners within 45 minutes of one town — booked out by referrals, with no idea what they'll be doing in ten weeks.

Figures are GBP on a UK/Ireland assumption, taken from "tradesmen". The structure holds for US, Canada and Australia — swap the currency and verify the local average job value before you quote anyone.

01

Why this trade and not the others

One question disqualifies most trades: does the customer need convincing, or just finding? Emergency plumbing, locksmiths, boiler repair — the customer is already sold and just needs a phone number. There is no funnel to build and no room for you to add value. Bathrooms need convincing.

What I ruled out, and why

  • Emergency plumbers and electricians — £150–£400 jobs bought at the moment of panic. No funnel exists to build.
  • Roofing and solar — where every new agency goes. You'd be the fourth pitch that owner heard this month.
  • Windows and conservatories — owned by national brands and lead resellers with budgets you can't out-spend.
  • Kitchens — a close second, and a legitimate alternative. Bigger tickets, but longer cycles, showroom competition, and a slower first win for an agency with no proof yet.

03

The person you're actually selling to

D
"Dave", 43
Owner · 3 crews · £900k turnover · 11 weeks booked

Time-served, went out on his own at 30. Genuinely good at the work and proud of it — the photos on his phone are better than anything on his website, which he paid £1,200 for in 2019 and hasn't touched since.

On site or quoting until five. Admin in the van. Quotes typed at nine at the kitchen table. His wife or an office admin answers the phone.

What keeps him up: he's booked eleven weeks out and has no idea what happens in week twelve. Three crews is £9,000 a month of wages going out whether the diary is full or not. Last January was frightening and he hasn't forgotten it.

His words — use these verbatim in ads and outreach

It's either feast or famine. Checkatrade leads are shocking now — same lead sold to five of us, all price-shoppers. I don't want to be the cheapest, I want people who want it done right. I had a marketing lad before. Took £800 a month, couldn't tell me what I got for it. I'm not being funny, but I haven't got time to be doing TikToks.

Where he is: Facebook — his personal profile more than the business page — local trade and community groups, Instagram if he's under 40, the merchant's trade counter at 7:15am, and WhatsApp constantly.

The pitch, in one sentence

"Four extra bathrooms a month is £36,000 of work and about £12,600 of gross profit. It costs you roughly £4,000 all in — my fee and the ad spend together. If I can't get you there in 90 days, I'll tell you before you have to ask."

That's an easy yes for a man with £9,000 of wages going out every month. It's also honest, which matters more — the numbers are wide enough to survive a bad first month.

06

Who to walk away from

  • Average job under £3,000. The maths does not work and no amount of skill on your part fixes it.
  • No capacity. One crew, booked six months out. You'd be selling him leads he can't service — he churns, and he tells people you didn't work.
  • Won't answer the phone. Leads sitting until nine at night triple the cost per sale, and it goes on your record, not his.
  • No photos of past work. Nothing to make ads out of.
  • Jack of all trades. "Bathrooms, kitchens, extensions, decking, bit of tiling." Nothing to position.
  • Price fighter. If he wins by being cheapest, ads just make him poorer faster.
  • Wants leads at £15 each because that's what the resellers charge. He's buying lottery tickets, not marketing. Educate once, then move on.
  • New-build subcontractor. His customer is a developer. He has no marketing problem for you to solve.
07

Where to find them

Ranked by how fast each one gets you a real conversation, not by how comfortable it is.

  1. Merchant trade counters, 7–8am. City Plumbing, Plumbase, trade desks. Unglamorous, and the highest-converting channel available to an agency with no track record. Everyone else is on LinkedIn.
  2. Checkatrade, MyBuilder, Rated People listings. Filter by trade and postcode. Anyone paying for those leads has already conceded that leads cost money — and is already unhappy with the ones he's getting.
  3. Local Facebook community groups. Watch for "can anyone recommend a bathroom fitter?" The firms named repeatedly are proven, referral-dependent, and exactly your profile.
  4. Instagram hashtags plus location. Good photos and under 2,000 followers is the perfect signal: he has the content and no distribution.
  5. Bathroom showrooms. They refer fitting work constantly. One showroom relationship introduces you to five fitters at once.
  6. Companies House. SIC 43.22 / 43.32 filtered by region, cross-referenced against filed accounts for turnover.
  7. Trade bodies — BiKBBI, Which? Trusted Traders, TrustMark directories.
08

Winning the first three with no case studies

You have no proof. Don't pretend otherwise, and don't discount your way in — a cheap price is the one thing you can never undo. Trade instead.

The offer

"I'll build and run the whole thing for one bathroom firm in this town, free for 60 days, and I'll come and film your next two jobs myself. All I want back is permission to use the results and a video testimonial if it works. After 60 days it's £X a month, or we shake hands and part ways."

It costs him nothing but access. It's visibly time-boxed. The filming is a real favour he can see. And the exclusivity — one firm in this town — is the line he'll repeat to other people, which is how your second client finds you.

The outreach that actually gets replies

A 90-second screen recording: his Facebook page, then a competitor two towns over running ads properly, then one specific thing you'd change. Send it with "Made you this, no pitch — happy to just send it over if it's useful." Give it away. The ones who reply are pre-sold.

Never open with

"I help bathroom fitters scale to seven figures." He's heard it, he's blocked the last four people who said it, and it doesn't sound like anyone he's ever met.

10

The funnel you build for him

  • Traffic — Meta, 25-mile radius, homeowners 40–65. A small Google search budget on bathroom fitters [town] for whoever is already in market.
  • Creative — before/after carousels, 30-second timelapses, walkthroughs with the fitter talking, and "what a £9,000 bathroom actually gets you." Price transparency is the highest-performing angle in this trade: the entire market hides its pricing and homeowners hate it.
  • Entry point — "Free design visit and a fixed written quote in 48 hours." Not "get a quote," which is what every competitor says.
  • Landing page — the promise, the fixed-price guarantee, ten before/afters, three named local testimonials, the process in four steps, a form with four fields.
  • Qualifier — budget band, property type, timescale, owns or rents. Filters the tyre-kickers out before they eat his evenings.
  • Follow-up — SMS in five minutes, call in fifteen. Then seven days: design guide → job story → what bathrooms cost and why → proof → objections → book the visit.
  • After the survey — quote follow-up at day 1, 3, 7 and 14. Most fitters quote once and never follow up. This alone is often worth more than the ads.
  • Expansion — reviews on autopilot, a referral ask at handover, and a reactivation campaign to every quote he's ever sent that went cold.
11

One client per town

Not out of principle — because it's simultaneously your best sales lever, your best retention lever, and the thing that forces you to expand rather than cannibalise. Say it on the first call: "I only work with one bathroom firm per area. If I take you on, I can't take your competitor."

Then move one town at a time. Client three in a new town is easier than client one, because by then you have local proof, local creative, and local benchmark data to show.

12

Validate it in seven days

Don't take my word for the trade. Test it before you build a brand around it.

  1. Days 1–2 — list 40 firms matching the spec from Checkatrade, Instagram and Companies House.
  2. Days 3–5 — record 15 personalised 90-second audits. Send them. Ask for nothing.
  3. Days 6–7 — count replies. Three or more out of fifteen means the profile is live — go build. One or two means fix the message, not the market. Zero almost always means you targeted firms that are too small.

Also open the Meta Ad Library and search bathroom advertisers in three towns near you. Few or no active advertisers isn't a warning sign — it's the opening, and it's exactly what you show Dave in the screen recording.

13

The second profile — later

Once bathroom fitters are working, add hard landscapers — driveways, patios, resin. Same buyer psychology, same £6k–£15k tickets, same before/after content engine, same funnel with different photos.

The reason it's these two and not any other pair is the calendar.
