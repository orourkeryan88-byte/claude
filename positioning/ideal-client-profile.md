# RELIER — Ideal Client Profile

> **Assumption:** figures are GBP and assume a UK/Ireland market, on the basis of "tradesmen". The structure holds for US/CA/AU — swap the currency and re-check the average job value locally before quoting anyone.

## The one-line ICP

> **Owner-operated bathroom installation specialists, £400k–£1.5m turnover, 4–15 staff, 3+ years trading, serving homeowners within a 45-minute drive of one town or city — who are booked out by referrals and word of mouth, and have no idea what they'll be doing in ten weeks' time.**

---

## Why this trade, over every other trade

| Test | Bathroom fitters | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Average job value | £6,000–£12,000 | Paid media only works when a job carries enough gross profit to absorb acquisition cost |
| Gross margin | 30–40% → £2,500–£4,500 per job | Max allowable CAC is huge. You can be mediocre and still be profitable for them |
| Sales cycle | 2–6 weeks, home survey required | **High consideration = a funnel is genuinely needed.** Emergency trades just need Google |
| Seasonality | Strongest Sept–Feb | Counter-seasonal to almost every other trade. Your retainers survive winter |
| Content supply | Before/after, timelapse, walkthrough | The single best predictor of ad account performance, and this trade generates it daily for free |
| Buyer demographic | Homeowners 45–65 | The heaviest Facebook demographic there is. Cheap reach, low competition |
| Market structure | Highly fragmented, thousands of 1–3 crew firms | No dominant national player owning the category |
| Agency saturation | Low | Roofing and solar are picked over. Bathroom fitters mostly still buy Checkatrade leads and complain about them |
| Referral dependence | Total | This is the pain. They have no control over next quarter |

**The disqualifying question for every other trade:** does the customer need convincing, or just finding? Emergency plumbing, locksmiths, boiler repair — the customer is already sold and just needs a phone number. There's no funnel to build, and no room for you to add value. Bathrooms need convincing.

---

## Firmographics — the spec

| Attribute | Target | Hard limits |
|---|---|---|
| Turnover | £400k–£1.5m | Below £250k they can't afford you. Above £3m they hire in-house |
| Team | 4–15 (2–4 fitting crews) | Under 2 crews = no capacity for the leads you generate |
| Years trading | 3+ | Under 2 years they're still learning to run jobs, not market them |
| Average job value | £6,000+ | **Below £3,000 the paid media maths fails.** Non-negotiable |
| Geography | One town/city + 45-minute radius | Dominate a patch, harvest the referral compounding |
| Existing marketing | Referrals, Checkatrade/MyBuilder, a neglected Facebook page | You want dormant potential, not an existing agency to displace |
| Decision-maker | The owner, on site or in a van | One person, decides in a day, no committee |
| Digital maturity | Low — has a phone full of job photos and no idea it's an asset | Your fastest possible win |

---

## The person

**"Dave, 43."** Time-served, went out on his own at 30, now has three crews and a unit with stock in it. Turns over £900k. Genuinely good at the work and proud of it — the photos on his phone are better than anything on his website, which he paid £1,200 for in 2019 and hasn't touched since.

**His week:** on site or quoting until 5, admin in the van, quotes typed at 9pm at the kitchen table. His wife or an office admin answers the phone. He has 400 photos and no system.

**What actually keeps him up:** he's booked eleven weeks out and has no idea what happens in week twelve. Three crews on the payroll is £9k a month of wages that goes out whether the diary is full or not. Last January was terrifying and he hasn't forgotten it.

**In his own words — use these lines verbatim in your ads and outreach:**
- "It's either feast or famine."
- "Checkatrade leads are shocking now — same lead sold to five of us, all price-shoppers."
- "I don't want to be the cheapest, I want people who want it done right."
- "I had a marketing lad before, took £800 a month, couldn't tell me what I got for it."
- "I'm not being funny but I haven't got time to be doing TikToks."

**Where he is:** Facebook (personal profile more than his business page), local trade and community groups, Instagram if he's under 40, the merchant's trade counter at 7:15am, and WhatsApp constantly.

## Why the maths works — run this in front of him

| Input | Value |
|---|---|
| Average job value | £9,000 |
| Gross margin | 35% |
| Gross profit per job | £3,150 |
| **Maximum allowable CAC** | **£3,150** |
| Lead → home survey | 50% |
| Survey → sale | 40% |
| Lead → sale | 20% |
| Realistic cost per lead (Meta, this trade) | £30–£80 |
| **Resulting CAC** | **£150–£400** |
| Cost as % of gross profit | 5–13% |

**The pitch, in one sentence:** *"Four extra bathrooms a month is £36,000 of work and about £12,600 of gross profit. It costs you roughly £4,000 all-in — my fee and the ad spend together. If I can't get you there in 90 days I'll tell you before you have to ask."*

That is an easy yes for a man with £9k of wages going out every month. It is also honest, which matters more.

---

## Disqualifiers — walk away

- **Average job under £3,000.** The maths does not work and no skill on your part fixes it.
- **No capacity.** One crew, booked six months. You'd be selling leads he can't service. He'll churn and blame you.
- **Won't answer the phone.** If leads sit until 9pm, your cost per acquisition triples and it will be recorded as your failure.
- **No photos of past work.** No raw material means no creative means no results.
- **Jack of all trades.** "Bathrooms, kitchens, extensions, decking, a bit of tiling." Nothing to position, no message to build.
- **Price fighter.** If he's winning work by being cheapest, ads make him poorer faster.
- **Wants leads at £15 each** because that's what the lead resellers quote. He's not buying marketing, he's buying lottery tickets. Educate once, then move on.
- **New-build subcontractor.** His customer is a developer, not a homeowner. He has no marketing problem to solve.

## Where to find them

Ranked by how quickly they get you a first conversation.

1. **Merchant trade counters, 7–8am.** City Plumbing, Plumbase, Victoria Plum trade desks. Unglamorous, and the highest-conversion channel available to a new agency. Everyone else is on LinkedIn.
2. **Checkatrade / MyBuilder / Rated People listings.** Filter by trade and postcode. Anyone paying for those leads has already admitted they'll pay for leads — and is already unhappy with them.
3. **Local Facebook community groups.** Watch for "can anyone recommend a bathroom fitter?" posts. The firms that get named repeatedly are proven, referral-dependent, and exactly your ICP.
4. **Instagram hashtags + location tags.** `#bathroomfitter #bathroomrenovation` plus your city. Good photos and under 2,000 followers is the perfect signal: they've got the content and no distribution.
5. **Bathroom showroom partnerships.** Showrooms refer fitting work constantly. One showroom relationship introduces you to five fitters.
6. **Companies House.** Filter SIC 43.22 / 43.32 by region, cross-reference turnover from filed accounts.
7. **Trade bodies** — BiKBBI, Which? Trusted Traders, TrustMark member directories.

## The wedge — winning your first three with no case studies

You have no proof. Don't pretend otherwise, and don't discount your way in. Trade instead.

**The offer:** *"I'll build and run the whole thing for one client in this town, free for 60 days, and I'll come and film your next two jobs myself. All I want in return is permission to use the results and a video testimonial if it works. After 60 days it's £X a month or we shake hands and part ways."*

Why it works: it costs him nothing but access, it's clearly time-boxed, the filming is a visible favour, and the exclusivity ("one client in this town") is the thing he'll actually repeat to other people.

**The outreach that gets replies:** a 90-second screen recording. His Facebook page, then a competitor two towns over running ads properly, then one specific thing you'd change. Send it with: *"Made you this, no pitch — happy to just send it over if it's useful."* Give it away. The ones who reply are pre-sold.

**Never lead with:** "I help bathroom fitters scale to 7 figures." He's heard it, he's blocked the last four people who said it, and it doesn't sound like anyone he knows.

## What you sell them

| Component | Price |
|---|---|
| Setup and build (funnel, tracking, first creative shoot) | £750–£1,500 one-off |
| Monthly management | £1,200–£2,500 |
| Ad spend (paid direct to the platforms by him) | £1,500–£3,000 |
| **His all-in monthly cost** | **£2,700–£5,500** |
| Break-even for him | **One job a month** |

Sell the outcome as a diary, not as leads: *"a full diary twelve weeks out, that you control."* He doesn't want leads. He wants to stop worrying about January.

---

## The funnel you build for him

- **Traffic:** Meta advantage+ and interest-led campaigns, 25-mile radius, homeowners 40–65. Small Google search budget on `bathroom fitters [town]` for the already-in-market.
- **Creative:** before/after carousels, 30-second timelapses, walkthrough with the fitter talking, "what a £9k bathroom actually gets you" price-transparency videos. **Price transparency is the highest-performing angle in this trade** — the whole market hides its pricing and homeowners hate it.
- **Entry point:** *"Free design visit + fixed written quote in 48 hours."* Not "get a quote" — every competitor says that.
- **Landing page:** the promise, the fixed-price guarantee, ten before/afters, three named local testimonials, the process in four steps, a form with four fields.
- **Qualifier:** budget band, property type, timescale, whether they own the property. Filters out the tyre-kickers before they eat his evenings.
- **Follow-up:** SMS in 5 minutes, call in 15. Then a 7-day sequence: the design guide → a job story → "what makes bathrooms cost what they cost" → proof → objection handling → book the visit.
- **After the survey:** quote follow-up at day 1, 3, 7, 14. **Most fitters quote and never follow up once.** This alone is often worth more than the ads.
- **Expansion:** reviews on autopilot, a referral ask at handover, and a reactivation campaign to every quote he's ever sent that went cold.

---

## Geography and exclusivity

**One client per town.** Not out of principle — because it's your best sales lever, your best retention lever, and it forces you to expand rather than cannibalise. Say it in the first conversation: *"I only work with one bathroom firm per area. If I take you on, I can't take your competitor."*

Then go one town at a time. Client three in a new town is easier than client one, because by then you have local proof, local creative, and local benchmark data.

---

## Validate this in 7 days before you commit

1. **Day 1–2:** list 40 firms matching the spec above from Checkatrade, Instagram and Companies House.
2. **Day 3–5:** record 15 personalised 90-second audits. Send them. Ask for nothing.
3. **Day 6–7:** count the replies. **Three or more replies out of fifteen means the ICP is live** — go build. One or two means fix the message, not the market. Zero means you're targeting the wrong size of firm, and it's almost always because they're too small.

Also check the Meta Ad Library for "bathroom" advertisers in three towns near you. Few or no active advertisers is not a warning sign — it's the opportunity, and it's what you show Dave in the screen recording.

---

## The seasonal pair — your second ICP, later

Once bathroom fitters are working, add **hard landscapers — driveways, patios, resin** as ICP number two.

Same buyer psychology, same job values (£6k–£15k), same before/after content engine, same funnel with different photos. Critically, their season is **March to September** — the exact inverse of bathrooms.

Run both and your agency has a flat revenue line all year while every competitor has a cliff in January. That is the whole reason to pick these two and not any others.

**Do not add the second ICP until you have three paying bathroom clients and a repeatable playbook.** Two niches at zero clients is not diversification, it's dilution.
