# System 1 — Lead Generation

```
CREATE & POST ADS → DIRECT MESSAGES → APPOINTMENT SETTING → BOOKED CALL → SALES
      creative           no friction        qualify              present
      = KING            build trust        & book               & close
```

**The job of this system:** turn strangers into booked calls without a landing page, a lead magnet, or a form. The DM *is* the funnel.

**Why DM-first and not a landing page:** you're selling to men who run crews, answer their own phones, and check Instagram in the van. A landing page asks them to fill in a form and wait. A DM asks them to say one word. Friction is the enemy at this stage — you can qualify hard later, once they're talking.

You run two lanes at once. **Outbound** is how you get clients one to three; **inbound** is how you get four onward.

---

## Lane A — Outbound (start here, week one)

You already have 22 named Dublin and Leinster firms in `prospecting/`. Outbound to a known list beats paid traffic when you have no case studies, because you can be specific.

### The asset: a 90-second screen recording
Not a pitch, not a deck. Record with Loom or your phone:

1. **0:00–0:15** — their Facebook page or website on screen. "Hi Dave, Ryan here. I was looking at your site this morning."
2. **0:15–0:45** — Meta Ad Library open, showing a competitor two towns over running ads. "This crowd in Swords are running four ads right now. You're running none."
3. **0:45–1:15** — one specific thing you'd change. Their price page, their photo gallery, their contact form.
4. **1:15–1:30** — "No pitch, just thought it might be useful. If you want the other two things I spotted, say the word."

### The send
Email or Instagram DM, subject line: *the firm's own name*. Nothing else.

> Dave — made you a 90-second video about your site, no pitch attached.
> [link]
> If it's useful I'll send the other two things I spotted. If not, ignore me and I won't chase.
> Ryan

**The "I won't chase" line does the heavy lifting.** It removes the thing they're bracing for and it's true — you don't chase, you send a second video in ten days instead.

### Cadence per prospect
| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| 0 | Screen recording sent |
| 3 | One line: "Did that come through alright?" |
| 10 | Second recording — a different angle, no reference to the first |
| 24 | Phone call to the listed number, mid-morning between jobs |
| 45 | Move to the long list, one email a quarter |

Twenty-two prospects, worked properly, is more than enough to land three clients. Do not add a 23rd until you've run all 22 through day 24.

## Lane B — Inbound DM ads (once you have one case study)

### Campaign setup
- **Objective:** Messages, destination Instagram Direct or Messenger
- **Geography:** Republic of Ireland, 25-mile radius of Dublin
- **Audience:** broad, plus interests — Checkatrade, construction management, Builders Merchants, small business owner. Let the algorithm work; the creative does the targeting.
- **Budget:** €20–€40/day to start. Below €20 you learn nothing.
- **Placement:** Reels and Stories first. That's where trade owners actually are.

### Six ad concepts, in test order

**1. The feast-or-famine hook** *(problem/agitate — run this first)*
> "You're booked till March. Grand. What are you doing in April?"
> Straight to camera, 20 seconds, no music. Name the fear they don't say out loud.

**2. The Ad Library reveal** *(mechanism)*
> Screen recording of the Meta Ad Library filtered to Ireland, attic conversions. Show how few firms are advertising. "There are eleven attic firms in North Dublin. Two of them are running ads. That's the whole opportunity."

**3. Price transparency** *(contrarian — the strongest angle in this trade)*
> "Every attic company in Dublin hides their price. One doesn't. Guess which one gets the calls."

**4. The €15 lead** *(objection)*
> "If you're buying leads at €15 a go and five other lads got the same one — you're not buying leads, you're buying lottery tickets."

**5. Proof-led** *(once you have a case study — becomes your best performer)*
> "This firm in Drogheda went from referrals only to eleven booked surveys in six weeks. Here's exactly what we ran."

**6. The capacity question** *(qualifier disguised as a hook)*
> "Don't ring me if you can't take on more work. Genuinely. I've had two lads sign up who couldn't fit the jobs in and it was a disaster for both of us."
> This one filters and builds trust simultaneously. Counter-intuitive ads outperform because nobody else runs them.

### Creative rules
- Shot on a phone, vertical, no agency polish. Polish reads as "expensive" to this audience.
- Captions burned in — most watch on mute.
- First frame is a face or a screen, never a logo.
- 20–40 seconds. Under 15 and there's no argument; over 45 and they're gone.

## Stage 2 — Direct Messages

The rule: **no friction, build trust, start conversations.** You are not selling in the DM. You're finding out if they're worth a call.

### Opening reply (within 5 minutes — automate the first line, then go manual)

> Thanks for messaging. Before I say anything useful — are you actually looking to take on more work at the minute, or just having a look?

Opening with permission to say no is what makes them answer honestly. Everyone else opens with a pitch.

### The four qualifying questions — ask them one at a time, never as a list

1. **Capacity.** "How many jobs a month are you doing now, and how many could you handle?"
2. **Job value.** "What's an average job for you, roughly?"
3. **Current source.** "Where's the work coming from at the moment?"
4. **Follow-up speed.** "When someone rings, who picks up?"

Never paste all four at once. A wall of questions in a DM reads like a form and gets ignored.

### Reading the answers against the profile
| Answer | Read |
|---|---|
| "Booked out, couldn't take more" | Not now. Ask when their diary opens and diarise it. Do not sell. |
| Average job under €6,000 | The maths doesn't work. Say so honestly and move on. |
| "All word of mouth" | **Ideal.** Dormant potential, no agency to displace. |
| "Tried Facebook ads, waste of money" | Ask what they ran and for how long. Usually two weeks and no tracking — an easy, honest correction. |
| "The wife takes the calls" | Good. There's a human answering. Speed-to-lead is fixable. |
| Already has an agency | Ask what they're getting. Either they're happy — walk away cleanly — or you've just found your opening. |

### The transition to a call

> Right, that all stacks up. I'd rather show you than type it — 20 minutes on a call, I'll walk you through what I'd run for you specifically and you can tell me if it's mad. No pitch deck, no pressure. Does Thursday morning suit?

Offer **two specific slots**, not a calendar link. Trade owners don't open calendar links from a phone on a building site. Once they agree a time verbally, *then* send the booking link to confirm it.

---

## Stage 3 — Appointment Setting

The handoff from "yes, Thursday" to a call that actually happens is where most agencies lose 40% of their pipeline. That handoff is System 4 — see `04-sales.md`. Do not treat it as an afterthought; it's the single highest-ROI system in the business.

---

## What to track weekly

| Metric | Target to start |
|---|---|
| Outbound recordings sent | 15/week |
| Outbound reply rate | 20%+ (3 of 15) |
| DM ad cost per conversation started | €8–€25 |
| Conversation → booked call | 25%+ |
| Booked call → showed up | 70%+ (System 4's job) |
| Showed → closed | 20–30% |

If reply rate is under 10%, the message is wrong. If conversation-to-booked is under 15%, you're pitching too early in the DM. If show rate is under 60%, System 4 isn't built properly.
