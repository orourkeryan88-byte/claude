RELIER · System 1 · Instagram

Getting calls booked through Instagram DMs

No ads. No cold email. You talk to them about their own work first, and the business conversation happens later — because they started it.

Part 1

Why this works

Just reading · 2 min

Their Instagram is their portfolio and they're proud of it. A finished attic with the light coming in, a dormer going on, the crew on a roof at eight in the morning. They post it because they're good at it.

Now think about who messages them there.

Customers asking for prices. And people selling them things. That's it. Nobody ever messages them about the actual work.

So that's the gap. You talk to them about the job. Not about marketing, not about leads, not about you. Just the work. And because it's the only message of that kind they've had all month, they reply.

The rule for the whole system

You never pitch in the DMs. Not on message one, not on message five.

You have one conversation about their work, and one question that happens to be about their business. If they want to know what you do, they'll ask. Those are the only ones worth talking to anyway.

Part 2

Fix your own profile first

Do this before you message anyone

The second you message someone, they tap your name. If your profile is empty you've lost them, and you don't get a second go.

  1. 1
    Put a real photo of your face as your profile picture.
    Not a logo. They're deciding whether you're a real person or a bot. A face settles it in half a second.
  2. 2
    Write a bio that says who you help, in their words.

Part 3

The five-day run for each firm

This is the actual system

You do not message on day one. You show up in their notifications first, so your name is already familiar when the message arrives.

Day 1
Follow them. Like their three most recent posts.That's it. Thirty seconds. Don't message.
Day 2
Leave one real comment on a job photo.Specific, not "great work". Something like: "That's a serious bit of steel work for a dormer — how long did that one take you?" He'll usually reply to the comment itself.
Day 3
Reply to one of their stories, if they post them.Story replies land straight in the DMs and feel completely normal. "Ha, that weather" is enough. Now you're in his inbox without having sent a cold message.
Day 4
Send the DM.The one below. By now he's seen your name three times.
Day 8
If no reply — one more message, different angle.Then leave it. Two messages is persistence. Four is harassment.

The DM

Message one
Dave — been seeing your stuff come up, that dormer in Clontarf
was a lovely job. The way you got the light into it especially.

Random question, and no agenda behind it — do you get much work
off Instagram itself? Or is it all word of mouth?

Why this one works, line by line:

  • You name an actual job. Not "your work" — that dormer in Clontarf. He knows instantly you've actually looked.
  • You say something only a person who looked would say. The light. The steel. The tiling. One specific detail.
  • "No agenda behind it" answers the question he's already asking.
  • The question is genuinely interesting to him. Every trade owner has wondered whether the Instagram is worth the effort. Nobody's ever asked him.
  • And it's the qualifying question in disguise. His answer tells you exactly where his work comes from — which is the thing you needed to know anyway.

If he doesn't reply — day 8

Message two
No bother if you're flat out Dave. One thing you might find
useful either way — I had a look and there are nine attic firms
within ten miles of you. Two of them are running ads. Everyone
else is invisible.

Thought you'd want to know. That's me done, I won't keep at you.

Then stop. "I won't keep at you" is a promise — keep it. About one in ten reply to that message specifically, because it's the first one that gave them something.

Part 4

When he replies

Where most people ruin it

He said something back. Do not pitch. Have the conversation he thinks he's having.

Dave — been seeing your stuff come up, that dormer in Clontarf was a lovely job. The way you got the light into it especially.

Random question, and no agenda behind it — do you get much work off Instagram itself? Or is it all word of mouth?
Cheers man. Bit of both to be honest. Mostly word of mouth and Checkatrade but they're gone very dear
Ah I've heard that a lot lately. Are you finding the same lead is going to four or five lads?
Every time. All price shoppers
That'd drive you mad. Are you flat out at the minute anyway or would you take more on?
Booked till about March but after that I'd want more
— this is the moment —
Makes sense. That's actually what I do — I get lads like yourself booked out without the shared leads. Not pitching you here, you're grand till March. But if you want I'll show you what I'd run for you, 20 mins, and you can tell me if it's mad. No deck, no pressure.

Read what happened there. Three messages about his work and his frustration. He told you his lead source, his complaint, and his capacity — the three things you needed — without being asked a single sales question.

Then the pivot came with "you're grand till March" in it. You gave him permission to say no, in the same sentence as the ask. That's what makes it land.

What his answers actually mean

Booking it

Getting the time
Grand. Thursday morning or Friday after 5, whichever suits better?
Two times, never a link

Don't send a booking link into a DM. He's on his phone on a site and he won't open it.

Get him to say a day out loud first. Then send the link to lock it in — "grand, here's the link so it's in both our diaries." That's what starts your reminder system.

Don't go faster than this

Instagram will action-block you for following and messaging too many strangers in a day. It usually lasts 24 hours to a week and there's no appeal.

Ten to fifteen follows a day and three to five new DMs is safe. Slow is also better here — you cannot write a specific, genuine message about someone's work at volume anyway.

That reply rate is roughly double cold email, and it's because of the warm-up days and the fact you're asking about their work rather than pitching your own.

If nobody's replying

It's almost always one of two things.

Your message was generic. If it could be sent to any of the 22 firms without changing a word, it's not the message. Name the actual job, name the actual detail.

Or you skipped the warm-up. A DM from someone who followed you four days ago and left a decent comment is a different thing entirely from a DM out of nowhere. The three quiet days are doing more work than the message is.
