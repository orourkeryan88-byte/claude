The job of this system

Turn strangers into booked calls without a landing page, a lead magnet, or a form. The DM is the funnel.

You're selling to men who run crews, answer their own phones, and check Instagram in the van. A landing page asks them to fill in a form and wait. A DM asks them to say one word. Qualify hard later — never at the door.

Creative rules

Shot on a phone, vertical, no agency polish — polish reads as "expensive" to this audience. Captions burned in, most watch on mute. First frame is a face or a screen, never a logo. 20–40 seconds.

Stage 2 — The DM conversation

Opening reply — within 5 minutes
Thanks for messaging. Before I say anything useful — are you
actually looking to take on more work at the minute, or just
having a look?

Opening with permission to say no is what makes him answer honestly. Everyone else opens with a pitch.

Four qualifying questions — one at a time, never as a list

  1. Capacity. "How many jobs a month are you doing now, and how many could you handle?"
  2. Job value. "What's an average job for you, roughly?"
  3. Current source. "Where's the work coming from at the moment?"
  4. Follow-up speed. "When someone rings, who picks up?"

A wall of questions in a DM reads like a form and gets ignored.

The job of this system

Eliminate buyer's remorse. The 48 hours after someone signs is when doubt creeps in — he's told his wife he's spending €2,000 a month with a lad he met on Instagram.

Every hour of silence in that window is an hour for the decision to rot. Something lands in his inbox within 60 seconds of yes, and again every time he finishes a step. He should never be waiting on you.

Step 1 — Sales Intake Form

Commercial details only. You fill this in, not him.

Legal business name + trading name · company registration number · VAT number and status · billing contact (name, email, mobile) · billing address · signatory name and email (often a spouse or accountant, not him) · package · management fee · setup fee · ad spend budget · start date · territory granted · anything promised on the call that isn't standard.

Those last two matter most. Write the exclusivity area down now or you'll argue about it in month four — and log every off-menu promise, because that's what you'll be held to.

The job of this system

Stop no-shows, and make sure whoever does show is pre-sold and pre-qualified. A 40% show rate and a 75% show rate are two completely different businesses at the same ad spend.

Every step asks for a small action — watch this, reply YES, accept the invite. Each micro-commitment raises the cost of ghosting. People don't no-show because they lost interest; they no-show because nothing happened between booking and the call to make it feel real.

Why "reply YES" works: a two-second action turns a passive booking into an active commitment — and it tells you who's engaged. Anyone who hasn't replied YES by the 24-hour mark is your no-show risk. Ring them.

Why the reschedule link is prominent: offering the exit lowers the no-show rate. A moved call is live; a no-show is dead. Never hide it.

2 — The booking page

Above the fold: "20 minutes. I'll show you exactly what I'd run for your firm." Subhead: "No deck, no pressure. If it's not a fit I'll tell you on the call." Calendar embed immediately visible, never below the fold.

Below it, in order: the 6-minute VSL (problem → why referrals cap you → what the system is → a real example → what it costs → who it's not for), then case studies, then FAQs.

Before you have case studies

Replace the case-study block with "I'm building my first Irish case studies right now, which is why the first two firms get it at cost." Honesty converts better than a fake logo wall, and it sets up the wedge offer.

The seven FAQs to answer before the call

  • What does it cost? (Give a real range. Refusing to say costs you more calls than the number does.)
  • How long before I see leads?
  • Do I need to be on camera?
  • What if I can't handle the extra work?
  • I've tried Facebook ads before and it didn't work — why is this different?
  • Am I tied into a contract?
  • Do you work with my competitors? (One firm per area. Say it here.)

The close everyone gets wrong

After you ask, say nothing. The silence is uncomfortable for about four seconds and then he answers. Fill it and you've talked yourself out of the sale.

When he says "I'll have a think": "Course. Can I ask — is it the money, the timing, or you're not sure it'll work?" Then handle the real objection instead of the polite one. If it's timing, book the specific follow-up date before you hang up.

The moment he says yes, System 2 fires.
