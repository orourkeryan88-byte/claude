Part 1

Five things, in plain English

Read this · 4 min · nothing to click

HighLevel is five ideas wearing a hundred menus. Once these land, the rest is just finding buttons.

Contact
One person. Dave the attic fitter. Everything else hangs off him.
Custom field
An extra box of information stored on that person, that HighLevel doesn't give you by default.
"Average Job Value = €28,000" lives in a custom field. His email address doesn't — that's built in.
Tag
A sticker you slap on a contact. That's it. It has no meaning except what you give it.
You stick confirmed-yes on Dave when he replies. Later you search "everyone without that sticker" and ring them.
Pipeline & opportunity
The pipeline is a row of columns — New Lead, Qualified, Call Booked. An opportunity is Dave's card, sitting in one of them. You drag it along as he progresses.
Workflow
The only hard one. A workflow is: when this happens, do these things in order. Nothing more.
When Dave books a call → send him an email → wait 5 minutes → text him → stick a sticker on him.

Every workflow has exactly two parts

This is what a workflow looks like on screen. One trigger, then actions in a line.

That's the whole model. The + circles between boxes are how you add a new action. You click a +, a panel slides in from the right, you pick what you want, you fill it in, you save. Then you do it again. Eight times, and you've built a workflow.

The one word that trips everyone up

"Wait" is an action, not a setting. If you want a text to go five minutes after the email, you don't set a delay on the text — you add a Wait box between them. Time is a step, same as an email is a step.

When something doesn't work

Open the workflow and click the Execution Logs tab at the top. It shows every contact that entered, which step they reached, and where it stopped. Nine times out of ten the answer is: still on Draft, or the trigger had no filter, or a Wait was set to Time Delay instead of Appointment Time.
